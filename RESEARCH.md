# The Commons Machine

Knowledge, participation and collective learning in complex decisions

## Research proposal · November 2026

The Commons Machine.

Knowledge, participation and collective learning in complex decisions.

How can different ways of knowing become meaningful collective action?

## 01 · The inquiry

Knowledge is distributed. Authority is not.

People hold different parts of the knowledge needed for change. The research follows how contributions become representations, decisions and action.

My research began in VET curriculum change. Co-design raised questions about whose contributions matter and whether participation supports learning.

## 02 · Research questions

Who knows? Who learns? Who decides?

How do actors bring distributed knowledge into collective decisions, and under what conditions does participation support collective learning and meaningful influence?

Supporting questions: How is knowledge excluded? What barriers shape participation? How do mediators and tools shape decisions? What evidence distinguishes learning from compliance?

## 03 · Three cases

Different settings. A shared inquiry.

Select a case to explore the proposed focus. These are planned studies, not completed findings.

Each case needs a bounded decision process, a time frame and access. The meaning and organisational boundaries of B Economy still need clarification.

## 04 · Knowledge and power

Every representation makes a selection.

Maps, categories, indicators and digital models make some aspects visible while leaving others out.

I will examine professional expertise, lived experience, practical skills, cultural and environmental knowledge. Epistemic injustice, decolonial perspectives and design justice guide questions rather than predetermine findings.

## 05 · Participation

Being in the room is only the beginning.

Follow influence from defining the problem to reviewing consequences. Select a stage.

I will examine who is invited, absent or excluded; whose proposals are adopted; who controls resources; who can challenge decisions; and whether representatives are accountable.

## 06 · Barriers

The cost of taking part.

Explore barriers that may shape access, voice and continuity. These are analytical prompts to investigate in each case.

Refusal and withdrawal also matter. Participation should not be treated as a requirement that people must accept without disadvantage.

## 07 · Mediation

The people between worlds.

Formal and informal mediators connect actors, translate knowledge and help contributions travel. Their influence also needs scrutiny.

Formal: facilitators, officials, coordinators and professional representatives. Informal: trusted neighbours, community organisers, peer leaders and workplace mentors. Roles overlap. Ask about trust, filtering, accountability, dependencies and unpaid work.

## 08 · Co-design under scrutiny

Question the tools. Question the process.

Workshops, canvases, maps and prototypes shape what participants can say, imagine and decide. Open the critical lenses.

Co-design is both an object of study and a possible research practice. I will compare the promises of models with their operation in practice. A polished output does not establish influence.

## 09 · Collective learning

Change is visible. Learning needs evidence.

Experience → changes in understanding or skills → use in later decisions. Each link requires evidence.

Learning may circulate into shared practices. I will distinguish this from compliance, imitation, chance or resource changes. People may learn while lacking the authority to act.

## 10 · Palantir

Representations connected to action.

A separate documentary study examines how Foundry connects objects, properties and relationships with actions and permissions.

Official documentation establishes the documented architecture and claims. Independent research and institutional sources are needed for uses and consequences. The three field cases are not assumed to use Palantir.

## 11 · Research design

Understand. Compare. Then design.

Comparative case study provides the initial framework. A design and evaluation phase could follow in one selected case.

The unit of analysis is a bounded collective decision process. Compare observation, interviews, documents, artefacts and decision histories. Seek contradictory evidence and rival explanations. Document my influence as researcher or facilitator.

## 12 · A hybrid possibility

Human knowledge. Collective learning. Optional computation.

An approach participants can question and revise: its categories, goals, rules and actions. AI remains optional.

The alternative is not yet developed or validated. Provisional principles include preserving disagreement, tracing contributions, explicit authority, accountability and review of consequences.

## 13 · Contributions

A critical account. A testable proposition.

Empirical findings across cases; a critique of co-design tools; a framework for learning and influence; provisional design principles and potentially a tested intervention.

The Commons Machine names an inquiry. Whether its approach supports common governance must be examined. Next decisions: case access, scope and a feasible longitudinal design.

## 14 · Discussion

What would count as convincing evidence?

How can I identify collective learning and trace its role in decisions? How can I evaluate participants’ real influence and the work of mediators?

Questions for Professor Avouris: Which indicators and research methods would strengthen the distinction between learning and adjustment? How can facilitators support revisable representations without concentrating power?
